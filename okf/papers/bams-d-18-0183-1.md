# Cross-Sector Management of Extreme Heat Risks in Arizona

*Guyer, H. E., Putnam, H. F., Roach, M., Iñiguez, P., & Hondula, D. M. (2019). Cross-sector management of extreme heat risks in Arizona. Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society, 100(3), ES101-ES104. https://doi.org/10.1175/BAMS-D-18-0183.1*

## Summary

This Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society meeting summary describes the Second Annual Arizona Extreme Heat Planning Workshop (26 March 2018, Scottsdale, AZ), which convened nearly 100 participants from approximately 40 organizations across public health, government, utilities, media, and academia to review heat-fatality cases and improve cross-sector collaboration. Anchored by Arizona Republic narratives of 30 individual heat deaths and the statistic of 1,193 excessive-heat deaths in Arizona during 2006-2016, participants identified risk factors (e.g., preexisting conditions, homelessness, social isolation, lack of air conditioning, with over half of Phoenix-area heat deaths related to indoor exposure) and generated programmatic intervention ideas. The workshop emphasized that heat governance responsibilities remain unclear and that systems-level, policy-supported coordination is needed.

## Conclusions

The workshop succeeded in building connections among individuals and agencies working on extreme heat, but gaps remain in understanding best practices for heat preparedness and response, partly due to a lacking evidence base on intervention efficacy. The authors conclude that using real case studies helped stakeholders think critically about known and novel interventions and who is best positioned to lead them, and they call for developing, sharing, and evaluating best practices, a central heat-relief project database, and stronger cross-sector and policy-level collaboration.
