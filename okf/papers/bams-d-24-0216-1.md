# Coproducing Opportunities to Advance Heat Resilience in Southern Arizona

*Roy, M., Keith, L., Arora, M., Luna, F., & Robinson, J. (2024). Coproducing opportunities to advance heat resilience in Southern Arizona. Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society, 105(11), E2070-E2077. https://doi.org/10.1175/BAMS-D-24-0216.1*

## Summary

This BAMS Meeting Summary reports the Southern Arizona Heat Planning Summit (Tucson, 3 February 2024), where over 110 representatives from grassroots, nonprofit, government, private-sector, labor, utility, and academic organizations coproduced heat-resilience priorities through facilitated breakout sessions on public health/healthcare, community/neighborhood action, energy/grid, built environment, and workforce protection. The summit was motivated by escalating extreme heat in the arid Southwest, including a record 176 heat-related fatalities in Pima County in 2023 and a projected rise to 182 days per year above 100°F under a high-emissions scenario, with disproportionate impacts on unhoused people, undocumented migrants, and outdoor workers. Participant input was coded into successes, gaps, and needs and synthesized into seven cross-cutting priorities across heat management, mitigation, and governance.

## Conclusions

The authors conclude that the first-of-its-kind Southern Arizona Heat Planning Summit successfully catalyzed cross-sectoral heat-resilience planning, with recommendations rapidly incorporated into Arizona's Extreme Heat Preparedness Plan (2024) and the City of Tucson's Tucson Resilient Together roadmap, and operationalized through measures such as solar-powered COOLtainers, expanded cooling-center networks, weekly EMAP Heat Briefings, bilingual outreach, and a Heat Workforce Safety procedure. They emphasize equity-centered governance directing resources to vulnerable groups and present the summit as a replicable model for other academic and local partners working to advance extreme-heat resilience in arid Southwest communities.

## Relevance

**High.** The paper is squarely within SCORCH's geographic and thematic scope: an arid U.S. Southwest setting (Pima County/Tucson), an extreme-heat exposure focus, explicit identification of vulnerable communities, and an emphasis on coproduced, sustainable climate-resilience solutions and adaptations (objectives 1, 2, 4, and 6). SCORCH itself is named as a supporting partner and funder, and the summit model is presented as transferable to other arid-region communities. It is rated High despite being a qualitative meeting summary rather than a quantitative epidemiological study, because it documents primary regional resilience actions and outcomes.
