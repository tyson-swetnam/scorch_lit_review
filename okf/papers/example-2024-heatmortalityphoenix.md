# EXAMPLE (synthetic) — Extreme heat and all-cause mortality in metropolitan Phoenix, Arizona

> **Example record** — synthetic, for demonstration. Not a real extraction.

*Example, A., & Sample, B. (2024). Extreme heat and all-cause mortality in metropolitan Phoenix, Arizona [Synthetic example record]. Journal of Arid Climate Health, 12(3), 200-215.*

## Summary

Across 2010-2022 in Maricopa County (~4.5M residents), extreme-heat days were associated with a 4% per-degree increase in all-cause mortality (RR 1.04, 95% CI 1.02-1.06), with the burden concentrated among adults 65+ and low-income outdoor workers.

## Conclusions

Extreme heat is a significant and inequitable driver of mortality in arid Phoenix; expanding cooling-center access is associated with reduced risk and represents a viable adaptation.

## Key effect estimates

Each 1 degree C increase in daily Tmax above the 95th percentile was associated with a relative risk of all-cause mortality of RR = 1.04 (95% CI: 1.02-1.06).
